SVD Deep Dive

Everything behind the pick

From the raw rating table to one recommended film, every idea and every line of maths this project uses.

1 · The problem

We have 5 users × 8 movies = 40 cells, but only 29 hold a rating. The other 11 are blanks — movies nobody has watched yet. A recommender must guess those blanks and suggest the film with the best guess.

Real services have millions of users and thousands of films, with over 99% of cells empty. That emptiness is called sparsity, and SVD is one classic way to see through it.

2 · The core idea

People do not rate movies at random. A few hidden tastes — say "big action spectacle" or "emotional romance" — explain most ratings. SVD finds those hidden tastes automatically from the numbers alone, with no genre labels needed.

Each user becomes a short list of scores on those tastes, each movie becomes a short list of how much it contains of them, and a rating is roughly how well the two lists line up.

3 · The decomposition

A (m×n) = U (m×m) · Σ (m×n) · Vᵀ (n×n)
  • A — the rating matrix. Rows are users, columns are movies.
  • U — orthogonal matrix. Each column is a "taste pattern" across users; entry (i, k) says how strongly user i expresses taste k.
  • Σ — diagonal matrix of singular values σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ … ≥ 0. A big σ means that taste explains a lot of the ratings.
  • V — orthogonal matrix. Each column is a taste pattern across movies; entry (j, k) says how much movie j carries taste k.

Where it comes from: the columns of V are eigenvectors of AᵀA, the columns of U are eigenvectors of AAᵀ, and σᵢ = √λᵢ (square roots of the shared eigenvalues). Written as a sum, the same thing is:

A = σ₁·u₁v₁ᵀ + σ₂·u₂v₂ᵀ + σ₃·u₃v₃ᵀ + …

Each term σₖ·uₖvₖᵀ is a rank‑1 "layer" of the ratings. Layers are sorted from most important to least.

4 · Why keep only k = 2

The Eckart–Young theorem proves that keeping the top k layers gives the best possible rank‑k approximation of A (smallest total squared error). Dropping the small layers also throws away noise and one‑off quirks.

Â = σ₁u₁v₁ᵀ + σ₂u₂v₂ᵀ

With just 5 users, k = 2 captures the two dominant tastes without memorising individual ratings. Too small a k underfits; too large a k copies the data and predicts nothing new.

5 · The pipeline in this project

  1. Fill blanks. SVD cannot work with holes, so each blank gets that user's own average rating.
  2. Find the top layer with power iteration: start with a guess vector v, repeat v ← AᵀAv and normalise 200 times. It converges to the strongest direction. Then σ = ‖Av‖ and u = Av / σ.
  3. Deflate. Subtract σuvᵀ from the matrix so the next strongest layer becomes the biggest, and repeat once more.
  4. Add the layers to get the prediction matrix Â.
  5. Recommend. For a user, look only at movies they have not rated, take the highest value in Â, clamp it to 1–5, and show it. Match % = prediction ÷ 5.

7 · Reading the result

  • σ₁ is usually large because all ratings are positive numbers: it mostly captures "how generously ratings are given overall".
  • σ₂ and later layers carry the real differences in taste between groups of users.
  • Reconstruction error (RMSE) on the known ratings tells how faithfully the rank‑2 model reproduces what people actually said. Lower is closer.
  • Predictions on blanks are estimates, not facts — they are the model's best guess.

8 · Limits and upgrades

  • Mean filling is a simple trick that biases blanks toward the average; real systems fit only the known cells instead.
  • Tiny data: 5 users cannot show reliable patterns; this is a teaching model.
  • Cold start: a brand‑new user or film has no ratings, so SVD has nothing to learn from.
  • Upgrades: Funk‑SVD with gradient descent, ALS, regularisation, user/item bias terms, and hybrid models with genre data.

Glossary

LATENT FACTORA hidden taste dimension found by the maths, not named by a human.
SINGULAR VALUEThe strength (σ) of one layer; bigger means more of the ratings explained.
ORTHOGONALColumns at right angles, so each taste pattern is independent of the others.
LOW‑RANKBuilt from only a few layers, so it is a compressed version of the original.
DEFLATIONSubtracting a found layer so the next one can be discovered.
SPARSITYThe share of empty cells in the rating matrix.
RMSERoot mean squared error: the typical size of the prediction miss.
COLD STARTThe problem of recommending for users or items with no history.

About

Math, made cinematic

MathsWood is a B.Tech mathematics mini project that applies linear algebra to a real problem. Missing ratings are estimated with a rank-2 SVD of the user–movie matrix, so recommendations come from hidden taste dimensions rather than simple averages. Every step — decomposition, truncation, reconstruction and selection — is computed live in the page, with no libraries.
